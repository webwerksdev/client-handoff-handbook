Three golden rules

  1. Edit content in the WordPress admin, not in Bricks. Almost everything (page text, images, blog posts, specials, FAQs, products) is edited through normal WordPress screens and custom fields. If you find yourself opening the Bricks editor for a routine text change, there's probably an easier (and safer) field for it.
  2. Bookend your edits with backups. Take a manual GridPane backup before you start an editing session and another after you've finished and confirmed the site looks right (the backups section). Each one takes about a minute, and together they turn any mistake into a non-event: the "before" is your undo button, the "after" preserves your finished work.
  3. When in doubt, stop before you click. Anything in the "hands-off zones" (the hands-off zones) is developer territory; send it to Lakeline Studio (how to reach us). A two-minute question costs far less than a repair job.

Your login and roles

Add /wp-admin to the site

Log in at cedarsalt.example/wp-admin. Bookmark it. Forgot your password? Click Lost your password? on the login screen; if the email never arrives, ask Lakeline Studio for a reset link.

One person, one login

Everyone gets their own account, so the site's history shows who changed what, and one person can be switched off without locking out everyone. Ask Lakeline Studio to add or remove people.

Security basics

Use a long, unique password, ideally from a password manager. Be wary of emails "from WordPress" or "from your host" asking you to log in; type the address yourself instead. And tell Lakeline Studio the day someone leaves, so their login comes out the same day.

Your admin menu, decoded

Everyday edits, step by step

Publish a blog post

  1. Go to Posts → Add New. Write the title and body in the standard editor.
  2. Set a featured image (right sidebar): the thumbnail on the archive and the image used when the post is shared. Landscape images work best.
  3. Pick a category so the post files correctly.
  4. Scroll to the SEOPress panel below the editor and fill in the meta title and description (the SEO section).
  5. Click Preview to check it, then Publish. The post appears on /blog/ automatically.

Add or update a seasonal special

  1. Go to Specials in the admin sidebar. Click Add New (or open an existing entry).
  2. Fill in the labeled fields: name, description, price note, and photo.
  3. Tick Active; active specials show on the Menu page automatically.
  4. Publish, then check the live page.
Tip

Deactivate specials instead of deleting them; last winter's special is one click from returning next year.

Update page content (custom fields)

  1. Go to Pages and open the page you want to change.
  2. Instead of one big text box, you'll see labeled custom fields grouped by section of the page: headlines, paragraphs, image slots, stats. Edit the field you need.
  3. Click Update, then view the live page to confirm. The design applies itself; you only supply the words and images.
Careful

Keep replacement text roughly the length of what it replaces. The design flexes, but a headline three times longer than the original will look crowded; check the live page, including on your phone.

Working with images

  • Upload via Media → Add New, or directly from any image field.
  • Resize large photos before uploading (2,000px on the long edge is plenty; squoosh.app or Preview on a Mac both work); phone photos straight off the camera are huge and slow the site down.
  • Name files before uploading: june-cardamom-buns.jpg, not IMG_4821.jpg. The site uses filenames as a fallback description.
  • Give every image alt text (a one-line description of what's in it). It matters for accessibility and SEO, and the site has been built to keep this consistent.
  • When replacing an image in a field, match the general shape (wide vs. tall) of the one it replaces.
  • Videos go on YouTube or Vimeo, then get linked; never upload video files. PDFs are fine to upload and link.

Bricks Builder: the other 5%

Bricks is the visual builder the site's layouts were designed in. A few pieces of content live directly in those layouts, so occasionally you'll need to open it. It's powerful, which is exactly why the workflow below stays deliberately narrow.

The safe editing workflow

  1. Open the page under Pages, then click Edit with Bricks in the top admin bar.
  2. Click directly on the text or image you need to change. The panel on the left shows its settings; edit the text there (or double-click the text to type in place).
  3. Change content only: words, an image swap. Don't drag elements, don't delete them, and don't touch the Style tab.
  4. Click Save (top bar), then view the live page to confirm.
  5. If you click something and the panel shows settings you don't recognize, press Esc and don't save; nothing changes until you hit Save. If that edit still needs to happen, treat it as developer work (how to reach us).
Don't

Never delete or drag elements in the structure panel, edit the header/footer/menu templates beyond a text swap, or change anything under Bricks → Settings. These control the whole site at once.

The store

The shop runs on WooCommerce inside the same admin. Orders and products are everyday territory; the checkout's plumbing is not.

Orders

  • Orders arrive under WooCommerce → Orders and by email to orders@cedarsalt.example. Open one to see items, totals, and the customer's details.
  • When an order ships or is picked up, set its status to Completed.
  • Refunds start from the order screen (Refund button), which sends the money back through Square. Card numbers never live on the site; the processor holds them.

Products

  • Edit price, stock, photos, and descriptions under Products, never in Bricks. The product pages redraw themselves.
  • Retire a product by switching it to Draft, never Delete. Past orders reference it; deleting leaves holes in your own records.
  • After changing prices or adding a product, load the live page and click through to the cart once.
Careful

Prices, tax, and shipping settings are customer-facing money. Double-check amounts before saving, and leave WooCommerce → Settings (tax, shipping zones, payment connections) to Lakeline Studio.

SEO & meta info (SEOPress)

Every page and post has an SEOPress panel below its editor. Two fields matter day to day:

  • Meta title: the headline in Google results. Aim for 50–60 characters, ending in | Cedar & Salt Bakery.
  • Meta description: the sentence or two under it. Aim for 140–155 characters; write it like a one-line pitch for the page.

The panel shows a live Google preview and character counts as you type. Every existing page already has these written; mostly you'll just add them to new posts and adjust when a page's focus changes.

Careful

Don't change page URLs (slugs). Renaming /our-story/ to something else breaks every link and Google listing pointing at it. If a URL really needs to change, a redirect has to be set up first; that's a developer task (how to reach us).

Good to know

  • The sitemap lives at cedarsalt.example/sitemaps.xml and updates itself; nothing to maintain.
  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools are connected and show how the site performs in search.
  • Your traffic lives in GA4 at analytics.google.com under the Cedar & Salt account; worth a monthly glance alongside Search Console.
  • Legal pages (privacy, cookies, terms) are deliberately hidden from search engines; that's intentional, leave as is.

Tracking & the consent banner

The site ships with a consent banner that keeps non-essential tracking from loading until a visitor opts in. That's not decoration; state privacy laws treat trackers that fire before consent as a legal liability, and the site has been tested to load clean.

  • Never paste a tracking snippet anywhere: no analytics tags, ad pixels, chat widgets, or "just one line of code" from a vendor. Even in a post. One ungated tracker undoes the compliance work.
  • Want a new tool? (A pixel, analytics, chat, booking widget.) Send it to Lakeline Studio: we wire it through the consent tool, verify nothing fires before consent, and keep the record of what runs.
  • The banner's wording and behavior are configured; leave its settings alone, and tell us if it ever stops appearing for new visitors (test in a private window).
  • Legal pages (privacy, cookies, terms) maintain themselves via Termageddon; never edit their text by hand; hand-edits are overwritten and can put the pages out of step with the law.

Your domain and email

The domain is yours to renew

cedarsalt.example is registered at Namecheap under your account and auto-renews each July. Keep the card on file current: a lapsed domain takes down the website and your email the same day, and recovering one can take weeks.

DNS goes through Lakeline Studio

The domain's records live in Cloudflare and control where the site and your email point. Record changes (new mail provider, a verification code a vendor asks for) are quick for us and risky by hand; send them over instead of editing.

Email is separate from the website

Your mail runs on Google Workspace, not on the web server. Site maintenance never touches your inbox, and a site outage doesn't stop your email, or the reverse.

Email "from the website"

Form notifications and order receipts are sent by the site through Postmark. If they stop arriving, check spam first, then tell Lakeline Studio, and tell us when the person who should receive them changes.

Backups & restore (GridPane)

GridPane is the hosting panel at my.gridpane.com. Automatic backups run on a schedule, so there is always a recent copy of the site, but the habit that makes you bulletproof is the backup bookend: one manual backup before you start editing, and one after you've finished and checked your work.

Take a manual backup

  1. Log in at my.gridpane.com and go to Sites.
  2. Click cedarsalt.example to open its settings, then open the Backups tab.
  3. Click Backup Now and give it a name you'll recognize: before-team-updates-nov-2026 going in, after-team-updates-nov-2026 coming out.
  4. Wait for it to appear in the backups list, usually a minute or two. Done.

Restore from a backup

  1. Same place: Sites → cedarsalt.example → Backups. Find the backup you want.
  2. Use its restore action and confirm.
  3. The site rolls back within a few minutes. Check the live site afterward.
Important

A restore rewinds the entire site to that moment: every edit, post, and form entry made since that backup is gone. Fine for undoing this morning's mistake; not for undoing one thing from last week (a single post can usually be fixed from its Revisions instead). If you're unsure which backup to use, get a developer's eyes on it first — restoring the wrong one is the one mistake a backup can't fix.

Monthly maintenance (owned by your team)

The site is not under an ongoing care plan, so nobody else is watching updates and backups for you. A once-a-month rhythm on your calendar keeps it secure and fast:

  1. Backup first. Manual GridPane backup, named for the date (the backups section). Confirm the automatic schedule has been producing recent backups too.
  2. Update plugins at Dashboard → Updates, one at a time, not "select all". After each, load the homepage and one inner page.
  3. WordPress core, the theme, and Bricks itself get extra care. Update them last, one at a time, and click through every main page afterward. If anything looks off, restore the step-1 backup, screenshot what broke, and hold that update for Lakeline Studio.
  4. Spot-check the site: submit the contact form to confirm it arrives, click through the main pages, glance at the site on your phone. Then take the "after" backup.
  5. Glance at search: Google Search Console for red flags. Anything unfamiliar, screenshot it.

Licenses are yours to hold. Bricks, ACF Pro, and the other premium tools require active licenses purchased and renewed by Cedar & Salt; the full list is in your Website Owner's Manual. A lapsed license doesn't take the site down, but it silently stops delivering updates, and running unpatched premium plugins is how sites get compromised.

Keeping the site fast

The site launched scoring 90+ in all four Google PageSpeed categories, measured on mobile, and you can check that claim any time: paste cedarsalt.example into pagespeed.web.dev. Scores aren't static, though; they're spent by what gets added. The three things that protect them:

  • Image discipline. The sizing habits in the images part of everyday edits are most of the battle; one 8 MB camera photo on the homepage is the fastest way to lose ten points.
  • No raw embeds. A pasted YouTube player, map, or chat widget loads a megabyte of script before a visitor touches it and can cost 15–25 points on its own. Send embeds to Lakeline Studio; we add them with a lightweight treatment that loads on click and keeps the score.
  • Lean add-ons. Every plugin and third-party script spends performance budget on every page. Ask before adding one; often the site already does the thing, or can, without it.

Hands-off zones

These run the machinery of the site. Changing them can take pages down or quietly break things that are hard to trace; leave them alone, and route any change you need here through Lakeline Studio:

  • WPCodeBox / code snippets: custom code powering the specials feed, schema, and store tweaks.
  • ACF field settings (the ACF menu itself); editing field definitions can disconnect content from the design. Filling in fields on a page is always fine; this menu is not.
  • Theme files, Bricks settings, and Bricks global classes/styles.
  • Plugins: updating is fine (the maintenance section); installing, deactivating, or deleting is not.
  • Cloudflare and GridPane server settings; everything in GridPane outside the Backups tab.
  • User roles: new WordPress users or permission changes are Lakeline Studio requests.

Glossary

Admin
The private back end at /wp-admin where you manage content.
Post vs. page
Posts are dated items like blog posts. Pages are permanent, like About or Contact.
Custom fields
The labeled boxes on pages and entries (made with ACF). Fill them in; the design does the rest.
Featured image
The main photo of a post or entry: the thumbnail and share image.
Slug
The end of an address, like /about. Don't change these on live pages.
Category
The kind of post, shown on the archive and used for filtering.
Bricks
The page builder the site was designed in.
Template
A design reused for every entry of a kind like every product page.
Revisions
WordPress's saved history of a post: your per-post undo button.
Cache
Saved copies of pages that make the site fast, and can make changes slow to appear.
Backup
A saved copy of the whole site that can be restored.
Alt text
A written description of an image for screen readers and Google.
Consent banner
The popup that holds back tracking until a visitor opts in. Legally load-bearing.
Tracker
Any third-party script that watches visitors: analytics, ad pixels, chat widgets.
DNS
The domain's address book: it points the name at the website and your email.
Registrar
Where the domain name itself is rented and renewed.
WooCommerce
The store system: products, orders, and the checkout.
